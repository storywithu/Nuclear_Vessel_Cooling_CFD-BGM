### B. Methodology & Model Simplification
**Note on Analysis Approach:**
This project initially intended to perform a comprehensive CFD simulation of the primary coolant flow, specifically tracking the Heavy Water (D2O) circulation from the inlet port to the outlet port of the Nuclear Reactor Vessel. However, due to computational resource constraints and analysis software limitations, the methodology was strategically optimized to focus on Transient Thermal Analysis.
**Model Simplification**: The fluid domain was modeled as an equivalent solid medium to evaluate heat diffusion and thermal pathways, bypassing explicit Navier-Stokes fluid velocity fields.
**Assumption**: While explicit fluid velocity and convection currents within the core were abstracted, this approach mapped the primary thermal pathways and bottleneck zones driven by the 500W core heat load.

#### [notes]
**Thermal Bottleneck** : Zones where the temperature color gradient changes sharply (densely packed temperature gradients) or areas where heat is trapped and fails to dissipate smoothly.

### D. Thermal Analysis Results
**- Analysis Overview**: 
A transient thermal analysis was performed to simulate the thermal behavior for the first 20 seconds at 0.1s intervals. Due to the nature of solid-state modeling, convective flow effects were excluded, which led to observed numerical instability in the heat accumulation patterns during the later stages of the analysis. This has been identified as a technical key area for future improvement when integrating Conjugate Heat Transfer (CHT) analysis. ### E. Conclusion & Lessons Learned
- **Achievement**: Established a robust transient thermal analysis process and identified critical thermal characteristics using an equivalent solid model.
- **Troubleshooting**: Proactively addressed limitations in the simulation tool’s animation engine (e.g., frame interpolation errors) by implementing manual time-step control and external recording techniques to achieve high-quality visualization.

### 4. Engineering Insights & Interpretation
- **Flow Visualization**: Changed to thermal performance only
- **Thermal Bottleneck Analysis**: Identification of critical zones where the temperature color gradient changes sharply (densely packed temperature gradients) or areas where heat is trapped due to solid-state conduction limits.
- **Observation on Limitations**: The transient output highlighted the constraints of bypassing explicit fluid dynamics, serving as a baseline rationale for shifting toward future CHT approaches.

### 5. Critical Reflection & Future Advancement
- **Modeling Strategy**: A transient simulation was selected over a steady-state analysis to capture the dynamic thermal response of the reactor core during power ramp-up phases. This ensures that the system's thermal lag and peak transient temperatures—which are critical for safety assessment—are accurately evaluated.
- **Thermal Performance & Scope Evolution**: An Equivalent Solid Model was implemented to simulate the thermal behavior of the reactor coolant, bypassing explicit CFD fluid flow analysis. While the initial engineering framework envisioned explicit coolant channel optimization (Topic A), practical constraints led to this refined thermal-conduction focus (Topic B).
